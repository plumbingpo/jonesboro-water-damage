Attic Water Damage in Jonesboro

There are two ways an attic ends up wet. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. From below, household moisture rises and condenses on the cold roof sheathing if attic ventilation is poor. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. Our team in Jonesboro inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

Most attic jobs center on wet insulation. Heating and cooling bills go up because cellulose and fiberglass alike give up R-value when wet. Worse, soaked insulation holds water against the ceiling drywall and the framing for weeks. Saturated material comes out, air movers and dehumidifiers dry the wood, and any mold on the sheathing or rafters gets treated.

What It Looks Like

  • Dark stains or streaks on the rafters and the underside of the roof deck
  • Water droplets or rust on the tips of nails that poke through the roof sheathing
  • Closets on the top floor, or the attic, that smell musty
  • Rust, water marks or drips on ductwork and metal straps in the attic
  • Flattened, matted or discolored insulation that stands out from what surrounds it
  • Water dripping from, or staining around, a ceiling light or bathroom fan
How We Fix It

Our Attic Water Damage Process

1

Inspect and Find the Source

To learn whether you have a leak, condensation or the two together, we go over the roof deck, the vents, the exhaust ducts and each flashing point. How far it spread is shown by moisture meters and thermal imaging.

2

Take Out Wet Insulation

We bag and remove insulation that is moldy or saturated, which lets the drywall and wood beneath it dry. Rooms under the attic are protected, and the route in and out stays contained.

3

Dry the Wood

With dehumidifiers and air movers, the sheathing, joists and rafters return to normal moisture levels. Visible mold is cleaned from the wood and an antimicrobial is applied.

4

Correct the Source

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. When the attic has stayed dry, the R-value is restored with new insulation.

The Damage Timeline

First storm

Insulation Takes It

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. With no sign inside the house, nobody notices the leak and the roof stays unrepaired.

24 to 48 hours

Mold May Begin

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

A few weeks on

Stains Come Through

Repeated wetting saturates the insulation, and water gets down to the ceiling. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

After months

Wood Starts Rotting

Sheathing delaminates and loses its hold on the roofing nails. Rafters stay damp enough to decay, and mold covers large areas of the roof deck.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

A Falling Ceiling

Saturated insulation is heavy. Drywall that has been wet from above weakens at the fasteners and can drop suddenly, bringing wet insulation down with it.

Mold on Sheathing

Poorly ventilated attics often grow mold across the underside of the sheathing. It can cover a wide area before anyone goes up to look.

Bath Fans Venting Inside

A bathroom fan that vents into the attic instead of outdoors adds warm, moist air every day. On cold sheathing that air condenses, and the result is easy to mistake for a leaking roof.

Insulation That Stops Working

Compressed, damp insulation does very little insulating, and it may not recover after it dries. Energy bills climb, and the rooms below the wet area are hotter or colder than the rest.
